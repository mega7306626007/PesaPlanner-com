Lesson 02 · Borrowing

Know the full cost
before you borrow.

Fast access can still come with real costs. Slow the decision down long enough to understand the repayment and whether it fits your cash flow.

Ask these questions first

  1. How much will I receive, and how much must I repay in total?
  2. What are the interest, fees, taxes, penalties and other charges? When are they due?
  3. What happens if I pay late, miss a payment or want to repay early?
  4. Can I make the payment after essentials and other commitments, including in a lower-income week?
  5. Is the provider licensed for this product? Can I read the actual agreement before accepting?
Compare totals, not just instalments.

A small-looking instalment can hide a long repayment period or additional fees. Ask the provider to show the total amount payable and the repayment schedule in writing.

Map repayment against dates

Put the repayment date beside expected income dates and bills. If repayment depends on uncertain income, identify a backup plan before borrowing. Avoid taking a new loan simply to cover another loan without understanding the combined cost and consequences.

Keep a record

Save the agreement, disbursement amount, repayment confirmations and any communication about changes. If the figures do not match what you agreed to, contact the provider through its official support channel and keep the response.

Illustration only.

For a hypothetical KSh 5,000 loan, compare the amount received with every scheduled payment added together. The difference is the borrowing cost before considering any other consequences. Use the provider’s current written disclosure for the real figures; costs vary by product and customer.

This lesson is general education, not a credit recommendation. Read the provider’s current terms and check relevant consumer information with the Central Bank of Kenya. Related: build a flexible budget · make room for a savings goal.
