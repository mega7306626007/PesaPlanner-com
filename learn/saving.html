Lesson 03 · Saving

Saving when every shilling
already has a job.

“I should save” is a lovely thought. Then rent, shopping and the unexpected bill enter the group chat. Give the goal an amount and timeline that fit your real cash flow.

Give the goal a shape

  1. Name the purpose: school fees, an emergency buffer, equipment, travel or another priority.
  2. Choose a target amount and a date. If either is uncertain, label it as an estimate.
  3. Work backwards to a contribution that fits. For irregular income, decide what to set aside when money arrives rather than relying on a fixed monthly date.
  4. Choose where to keep the money based on access, safety, fees, terms and your own needs. Understand the product before using it.
Illustration only — adjust to your situation.

A KSh 3,000 target over 10 weeks would mean an average of KSh 300 per week if contributions were even. If some weeks are tighter, set a smaller minimum and add more when you can; the date may need to move.

Make it resilient

Start with a contribution you can repeat after essentials. A missed week is information: revisit the target, amount or timing. Keep short-term money goals separate from money you need for near-term bills.

Review the trade-offs

Before choosing an account or savings product, check access rules, charges, interest conditions and what happens if you withdraw early. Rates and terms can change, so confirm them directly with the provider. Do not put money you cannot afford to lose into an investment because of a promised return.

Celebrate progress without losing the plan

Record each contribution and check the remaining amount. If the goal changes—like a new semester cost—update the plan instead of quietly borrowing from another commitment.
