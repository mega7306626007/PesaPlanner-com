Lesson 01 · Budgeting

Build a budget
that bends.

A budget is a working plan for money—not a scorecard. Make it simple enough to update when income, fares, prices or plans change.

Start with what is known.

Use money already received or a conservative estimate. Keep uncertain income separate until it arrives.

Try a four-part plan

  1. Money in: list income by source and expected date. For irregular income, plan from what has actually arrived.
  2. Committed essentials: rent, food basics, transport, utilities, school costs and minimum debt payments.
  3. Flexible spending: airtime, outings, subscriptions and other choices. Give these a limit that still leaves room for real life.
  4. Next goals: savings, upcoming bills and extra debt payments. Assign an amount and a date where possible.

Use a weekly check-in

At the start of the week, compare available money with bills due before the next likely income. Midweek, record what changed. At week’s end, adjust next week’s plan without treating a missed target as failure.

Illustration only — not a recommended budget.

If KSh 6,000 arrives for the week, first set aside the rent and fare amounts due before the next payment. If those commitments total KSh 4,200, the remaining KSh 1,800 still needs to cover food, flexible choices and any goal contribution. The exact split depends on the person and household.

When money arrives in pieces

List due dates next to bills and income dates. Hold money for near-term commitments before treating the balance as available. A “safe to spend” estimate is only as complete as the bills and transactions you recorded—verify it before making a decision.

Keep the plan visible

Group spending into a few categories you can maintain. Track cash as well as mobile-money and bank activity if those affect your decisions. Review the plan after a rent change, new term, new job, or a change in household responsibilities.
